**Plumbing company software should run five workflows well: emergency calls, recurring service, estimates, job history, and payment collection. This guide gives owners a stage-by-stage checklist for each workflow, the features that actually matter at every stage, and an honest case for when buying a full platform is premature.**

## Start by mapping the five plumbing job types

**Most plumbing companies run five distinct job types, and each one stresses a different part of the software. Map your call mix before you compare tools, because a platform built for flat-rate service calls will frustrate a shop that lives on repipes, water heater installs, and maintenance agreements.**

Before a demo, count how your last 90 days of work split across these categories:

| Job type | Example | What the software must handle |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency service | Burst supply line at 9 p.m. | After-hours intake, live dispatch, on-site payment |
| Diagnostic and repair | Running toilet, low pressure | Flat-rate pricebook, photo capture, same-visit invoicing |
| Install and replacement | Water heater swap, repipe | Multi-option estimates, parts lists, follow-up scheduling |
| Recurring and agreement work | Annual water heater flush, commercial PM | Auto-renewal scheduling, agreement pricing, visit history |
| Warranty and callback | Leaking fitting from last month's job | Job history lookup, no-charge flags, callback tracking |

If 60 percent of your board is emergency and repair calls, dispatch speed and a clean flat-rate pricebook outrank everything else. If you carry 300 service agreements, renewal automation and visit history move to the top. The buying order changes with the mix, which is why generic "best software" lists mislead plumbing owners more than they help.

## Emergency call workflow checklist

**Emergency calls are where plumbing companies win or lose customers, and the software chain runs from first ring to collected payment in under three hours. The checklist below covers intake, dispatch, arrival, and closeout, with the software feature that makes each step hold together.**

1. **Intake.** The call gets logged with address, problem description, shutoff status, and photos if the customer can text them. Software feature: a call intake form or call integration that creates the job before the dispatcher hangs up. If your team still writes emergencies on sticky notes, start here.
2. **Triage and pricing frame.** The dispatcher quotes the dispatch or diagnostic fee and sets arrival expectations. Software feature: stored after-hours rates so the office never quotes from memory at 10 p.m.
3. **Dispatch.** The job lands on the right tech based on location, on-call rotation, and skill. Software feature: a live dispatch board with drag-and-drop reassignment. For a deeper look at this layer, the companion guide on [plumber scheduling software and holding emergency capacity](/guides/plumber-scheduling-software/) walks through how to reserve slack in the board for after-hours surges.
4. **On-site execution.** The tech sees the full address record, past work at that property, and the pricebook on a phone or tablet. Software feature: mobile access to job history and flat-rate pricing so the tech quotes a repair without calling the office.
5. **Closeout.** Invoice generated on site, card collected before the tech leaves, receipt texted automatically. Software feature: on-site invoicing with integrated card processing.

Run your last five emergency calls against this checklist. Every step where a human had to re-enter information, call the office, or wait until Monday to bill is a software requirement, not a nice-to-have.

## Recurring service and maintenance agreements

**Recurring work is the stabilizer in a plumbing business, and the software has to do three things: schedule agreement visits without manual rebooking, apply agreement pricing automatically, and keep a per-property service record that makes renewals easy to justify. Without those three, agreements decay into a spreadsheet.**

For residential shops, recurring work means water heater flushes, annual inspections, and drain maintenance plans. For commercial accounts, it means scheduled PM on backflow devices, grease traps, and restroom fixtures across multiple sites.

The workflow checklist for agreement work:

- **Enrollment.** The agreement is sold on site from the estimate, with the discount applied automatically. If the tech has to call the office to "add the plan price," the attach rate will stay low.
- **Scheduling.** Visits auto-generate onto the board weeks ahead, grouped by geography so a tech runs four flushes in one neighborhood instead of zigzagging the county.
- **Visit execution.** The tech sees the agreement terms, the property's equipment list, and last visit's notes before knocking.
- **Renewal.** The system flags expiring agreements 60 days out and triggers a renewal sequence, not a panicked phone call from the office.
- **Reporting.** You can answer two questions in one click: how many active agreements you carry, and what booked revenue they represent next quarter.

## Estimates, job history, and the customer record

**Good plumbing estimates present options, and good job history makes the next visit faster. The software should let a tech build a good-better-best estimate on site, convert the approved option to an invoice in one tap, and file every photo, note, and part against the property record permanently.**

### Estimates that present options

A water heater estimate should show repair, standard replacement, and tankless upgrade side by side with real prices, not a single handwritten number. The software requirements:

- A pricebook organized by task, with parts and labor bundled, so techs quote from the book instead of calculating in the driveway.
- Multi-option estimate presentation the customer can approve on the tech's tablet with a signature.
- One-tap conversion from approved option to work order and then to invoice, with no re-typing.
- A follow-up queue for unsold estimates. Illustrative example: a shop sending 40 replacement estimates a month that follows up on none of them is leaving real close-rate points on the table; even a two-touch text sequence recovers some of them.

### Job history as an operating asset

Job history is not an archive. It is how you handle the callback, quote the return visit, and prove warranty work. Every completed job should attach to the property record with:

1. Photos of the before condition, the install, and the serial numbers of anything set.
2. Parts used, tied to the pricebook line items.
3. Tech notes written in plain language a different tech can act on six months later.
4. Warranty terms and expiration dates.

The callback test: a customer calls saying the fitting you replaced in March is dripping. Can your office pull the March job, see the part, the photos, and the warranty status in under a minute, and dispatch a no-charge callback flagged as warranty work? If the answer involves digging through paper invoices or texting the original tech, that gap is a buying criterion.

## Payment collection and closing the cash gap

**The money workflow in a plumbing company runs from on-site card collection for service calls to net-30 invoicing for commercial work, and the software has to handle both without the office re-entering anything. The goal is simple: the job is not complete until payment is collected or the invoice is delivered and tracked.**

The collection checklist:

- **On-site service calls.** Card or tap-to-pay collected before the tech leaves the driveway, with the receipt texted automatically. Every residential service job that leaves without payment becomes a collections task that costs office time and closes at a worse rate.
- **Deposits on installs.** Larger jobs collect a deposit at estimate approval, applied automatically to the final invoice.
- **Commercial invoicing.** Invoices generate from the completed work order with photos attached, which cuts the "send us documentation" delay that stretches commercial receivables.
- **Aging visibility.** A dashboard showing what is outstanding, by how many days, with automated reminder sequences at the intervals you set.
- **Books sync.** Payments and invoices flow to your accounting system without double entry.

## When plumbing company software is the wrong move

**A full platform is the wrong purchase when your real problem is process, not tooling. A one-truck shop doing fifteen jobs a week with a working invoicing app and a shared calendar usually gets more from tightening its pricebook and follow-up habits than from a per-tech subscription it will not fully use.**

Watch for these signs that you should fix process first:

- Your pricebook does not exist in any consistent form, so no software can automate quoting yet.
- You have no defined on-call rotation, so dispatch features will just digitize chaos.
- Nobody owns estimate follow-up, so a follow-up queue will fill up and be ignored.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is plumbing company software?

It is field service software configured for plumbing work: call intake, dispatch, a flat-rate pricebook, mobile estimates and invoicing, on-site payment, and a permanent per-property job history. The plumbing-specific parts are the pricebook structure, emergency dispatch handling, and maintenance agreement management.

### How much does plumbing company software cost?

Most platforms price per user or per technician per month, with payment processing fees on top. Treat any specific dollar figure you see in marketing as a starting quote, and model the total against your headcount, including office users, before comparing vendors.

### Can one platform handle emergency calls and maintenance agreements?

Yes, and it should. The point of a single platform is that the emergency call, the estimate it produces, the agreement sold from that estimate, and the renewal visit a year later all live on one customer and property record. If a vendor handles emergencies well but has no agreement module, that is a real limitation for shops building recurring revenue.

### What should a plumbing company set up first when switching software?

The pricebook and the customer or property records, in that order. Dispatch and invoicing only work as well as the pricing and history underneath them. Import active agreements and recent job history before go-live so techs are not blind on their first week of calls.

### Do small plumbing shops need dispatch software?

A one or two truck shop can run on a simpler setup, but the moment you have three techs, an on-call rotation, and same-day rescheduling, a live dispatch board pays for itself in fewer phone calls and fewer double-booked windows. The trigger is coordination pain, not company size.

### How does plumbing software handle callbacks and warranty work?

The better platforms let you create a no-charge job linked to the original work order, so the callback pulls the original photos, parts, and notes automatically and stays flagged as warranty in your reporting. That linkage is what lets you measure callback rate instead of guessing at it.
